ABSTRACT 
We developed a numerical simulation algorithm to explore the nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) response of 
the porous media based on the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill (CPMG) pulse sequence and the Bloch equation. The 
evolution of the magnetization vector of two representative pores at different pulse properties, including the 
excitation angle, the refocusing angle, the phase angle, as well as the pulse duration are simulate to understand 
the NMR relaxation signals. The result showed that the normalized magnetization is symmetrical with the 
excitation angle and positive with the T
2 
spectrum ’ s amplitude when the excitation angle is less than 90
◦
. In 
additional, the refocusing angle has no clear influence on the NMR response. The phase angle of the excitation 
pulse is inversely correlated with the echo amplitude and can be neglected when the value is lower than 15
◦
. The 
phase angle of the refocusing pulse causes the zig-zag phenomenon, but the response of the even echoes is not 
disturbed. Moreover, the influence of the pulse duration should not be neglected at higher values, particularly for 
the mesopore. The simulation results are helpful for the design and optimization of the pulse sequence, and the 
data manipulation of the measured signals. 
1. Introduction 
The low field nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) technique has been 
widely applied in geological studies including the pore structure char -
acterization, porosity and permeability predication, hydrocarbon vis -
cosity estimation, wettability evaluation, as well as the fluid 
discrimination in recent decades ( Freedman et al., 1997 ; Coates et al., 
1999 ; Dunn et al., 2002 ; Freedman and Heaton, 2004 ; Song, 2010 ; Xie 
and Xiao, 2011 ; Xiao et al., 2012 ; Mitchell et al., 2015 ; Feng et al., 2016 ; 
YanHe et al., 2017 ; Ge et al., 2018 ; Liang et al., 2019 ). The NMR signal is 
contributed by the hydrogen in pore space, and is lithology-independent 
in paramagnetic free reservoirs. The most frequently observed param -
eters both in laboratory and borehole measurements is the transversal 
relaxation time (T
2
), generated by the Carr-Purcell-Meiboom-Gill 
(CPMG) pulse sequence ( Coates et al., 1999 ; Dunn et al., 2002 ). 
Despite tremendous successfully examples in using the T
2 
information 
for the reservoir evaluation, the measurement and process of low field 
NMR data to characterize the unconventional resources such as the gas 
shale and tight sand is still challenging (Bachman et al., 2007; 
Grunewald and Knight, 2011 ; Behroozmand et al., 2015 ; Washburn, 
2014 ; Mehana and El-monier, 2016 ; Sondergeld et al., 2016; Obasi and 
Pashin, 2018 ; Tan et al., 2019 ; Testamanti and Rezaee, 2019 ). Special 
precautions are necessary before the direct application of the NMR data. 
Literature reviews showed that great improvements have been made 
in the manipulation of the raw decay signals such as the noise reduction 
( Xie et al., 2014 ; Ge et al., 2015 ; Cai and Chen, 2016 ) and inversion 
algorithms ( Berman et al., 2013 ; Mitchell and Chandrasekera, 2014 ; Zou 
and Xie, 2015 ; Wang et al., 2017 ; Guo et al., 2018 ), and the mathe -
matical transformations of the T
2 
spectrum ( Ge et al., 2015 ; Hu et al., 
2016 ; Anand, 2017 ; Jiang et al., 2019 ). However, the forward modeling 
works to investigate the influence of the pulse sequence property are 
relative rare ( Hurlimann and Griffin, 2000 ; Borneman et al., 2010 ; Li 
et al., 2013 ; Casabianca et al., 2014 ). Pulse imperfections of the CPMG 
echo trains may introduce large artifacts ( Mandal et al., 2015 ; Konuma 
et al., 2018 ), whereas the intrinsic mechanism still remains ambiguous. 
This work focuses the influences of the pulse sequence on the NMR 
measurements and their inversion results for reservoir rocks with 
different pore size distributions through numerical simulations. We are

processing methods are not elaborated here. We just give some basic 
information of the Bloch equation, the CPMG pulse sequence, and the

homogeneity and the molecular diffusion are not discussed here.

3.1. Effect of the excitation angle 
Fig. 6 a and Fig. 6 b shows the relationship between the normalized 
magnetization of the first two echoes and the excitation angle. The 
normalized magnetization is symmetrical with the excitation angle (the 
value of θ is the same as 180- θ ). A positive correlation between the 
normalized magnetization and the excitation angle is observed when the 
excitation angle is lower than 90
◦
. Fig. 6 c – f shows the simulated NMR 
decay curves and their inversion results at different excitation angles. It 
is clear that the amplitude is positively correlated with the excitation 
angle, revealing that the influence of the excitation angle should not be 
neglected. However, the magnetization variation caused by the excita -
tion angle has weak influence on the T
2 
distribution. It is also noted that 
the amplitude of the T
2 
spectrum simulated at the excitation angle of 90
◦
is less than the forward micropore model, which is contributed by the 
decay during TE. 
3.2. Effect of the refocusing angle 
Fig. 7 depicts the simulated NMR decay curves and their inversion 
results with different refocusing angles. It is obvious that the refocusing 
angle does not contribute any influence to the NMR response. It is easy 
to interpret since the main function of the refocusing pulse is to rephase 
the protons and generate the measurable signal. It is applicable to reduce 
the refocusing angle as long as the phase angle is stable. For simplicity, 
the inversion results are not shown.

3.3. Effect of the phase angle 
Fig. 8 gives the simulated NMR decay curves and their inversion 
results for two pores using the standard CPMG pulse sequence but with 
different phase angles of the excitation pulse. It seen that the amplitude 
of the first and the second echo is negatively correlated with the phase 
angle. The decay curve and the corresponding T
2 
spectrum alters slightly 
when the phase angle is lower than 15
◦
, indicating that the influences of 
the phase angle can be neglected, both for the micropore and the mes -
opore. Fig. 9 shows the simulated NMR decay curves for two pores using 
the standard CPMG pulse sequence but with different phase angles of the 
refocusing pulse. The zig-zag phenomenon is observed for both pores 
when varying the phase angle. It is easy to explain that the time evo -
lution of the magnetization vector is controlled by the phase angle and 
the duration of the refocusing pulse. Moreover, we found that the signal 
of the even echoes is not affected by the phase of the refocusing pulse. 
Therefore, choosing the data of even echoes may overcome the zig-zag 
phenomenon (or the tremble) brought by the phase angle of the refo -
cusing pulse. 
3.4. Effect of the pulse duration 
Fig. 10 depicts the simulated NMR decay curves and theirs corre -
sponding inversion results for the micropore using the standard CPMG 
pulse sequence but with different pulse durations. It is shown that the 
variation of the NMR response is slight at low pulse duration. However, 
the T
2 
spectrum deviates from the forward model heavily when the pulse 
duration is larger than 20 percent of the echo spacing. Similar phe -
nomenon is also observed for the mesopore, as is indicated in Fig. 11 . 
Moreover, the mesopore is more sensitive to the pulse duration. 
4. Conclusion 
We developed a numerical algorithm to simulate the NMR responses 
of two representative pores based on the Bloch equation. The effects of 
excitation angles, refocusing angles, phase angles, pulse durations are 
investigated. Numerical tests on synthetic data show that the excitation 
angle is the controlling factor of NMR responses, but the refocusing 
angle is irrespective of the NMR response. The phase angle of the exci -
tation pulse is negative with the amplitude and poses slight influence at 
low values. Moreover, the phase angle of the refocusing pulse only af -
fects the amplitude of odd echoes, making it feasible to use the even 
echoes to reduce the inaccuracy. The effect of the pulse duration can be 
neglected with relative low values. However, its influence should be 
considered when the pulse duration is higher than 10% of the echo 
spacing, especially for the mesopore. However, much work should be 
done to incorporate the numerical simulation and the experimental 
result to verity our research. In additional, the effect of the off resonance 
and the inhomogeneity of the magnetic field are also to be investigated 
in future studies.
