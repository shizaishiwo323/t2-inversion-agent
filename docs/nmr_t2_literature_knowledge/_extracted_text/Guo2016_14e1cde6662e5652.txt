1. Introduction
Tight sandstone reservoirs have the characteristics of low 
porosity, low permeability and complex pore structure, 
making evaluating petrophysical properties of these reservoirs 
by conventional logging methods challenging. Therefore it 
is necessary to develop new well logging techniques such 
as nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) logging to overcome 
this challenge. NMR logging measures the relaxation and dif-
fusion information of the hydrogen in formation pore fluid, 
which can be used to obtain petrophysical parameters, such as 
porosity, permeability, pore structure, wettability and various 
fluids saturations (George et al 19 9 8, Dunn et al 2002, Chen 
et al 2003). However, one dimensional (1D) NMR logging can 
only make use of the distribution of transverse relaxation time 
(T2), when multi-phase fluids (water, oil and gas) coexist in 
porous media. T2 distributions of various fluids often overlap 
one another, making it difficult to identify the fluids from one 
another (Sun and Dunn 2002, Hurlimann et al 2002a, 2002b, 
Xie et  al 2005, Tan et  al 2014). Multi-dimensional NMR 
describes the spatial distribution of hydrogen protons by using 
multiple physical parameters, such as transverse relaxation 
time (T2), longitudinal relaxation time (T1) and diffusion coef-
ficient (D), which can overcome the disadvantage of 1D NMR 
in reservoir fluids identification (Heaton et al 2004, 2008, Xie 
et al 2009 a, Xie and Xiao 2009).

Abstract
Conventional logging methods have limitations in the evaluation of tight sandstone reservoirs. 
The multi-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) logging method has the advantage 
that it can simultaneously measure transverse relaxation time (T2), longitudinal relaxation 
time (T1) and diffusion coefficient (D). In this paper, we simulate NMR measurements of tight 
sandstone with different wettability and saturations by the random walk method and obtain 
the magnetization decays of Carr–Purcell–Meiboom–Gill pulse sequences with different wait 
times (TW ) and echo spacings (TE) under a magnetic field gradient, resulting in D-T2-T1 maps 
by the multiple echo trains joint inversion method. We also study the effects of wettability, 
saturation, signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) of data and restricted diffusion on the D-T2-T1 
maps in tight sandstone. The results show that with decreasing wetting fluid saturation, the 
surface relaxation rate of the wetting fluid gradually increases and the restricted diffusion 
phenomenon becomes more and more obvious, which leads to the wetting fluid signal moving 
along the direction of short relaxation and the direction of the diffusion coefficient decreasing 
in D-T2-T1 maps. Meanwhile, the non-wetting fluid position in D-T2-T1 maps does not change 
with saturation variation. With decreasing SNR, the ability to identify water and oil signals 
based on NMR maps gradually decreases. The wetting fluid D-T1 and D-T2 correlations in 
NMR diffusion-relaxation maps of tight sandstone are obtained through expanding the wetting 
fluid restricted diffusion models, and are further applied to recognize the wetting fluid in 
simulated D-T2 maps and D-T1 maps.
Keywords: tight sandstone, multi-dimensional NMR, random walk, wettability, restricted 
diffusion

Complex pore structure, wettability and various fluids 
satur ations of tight sandstone reservoirs are the key factors 
that affect their NMR response. The random walk method 
can be used to simulate the procedure of NMR measurements 
accurately and effectively, as well as to study the influence of 
individual factors on the NMR response. There has been quite 
a lot of previous research on simulating 1D and two-dimen-
sional (2D) NMR responses of sandstones and carbonate 
rocks; for example, Toumelin et  al (2004, 2007) simulated 
the magnetization decays of Carr –Purcell–Meiboom–Gill 
(CPMG) pulse sequences with different wait times (TW ) and 
echo spacings (TE) based on disordered packs of spherical 
grains, and systematically studied the influence of various 
factors on fluid identification in D-T2 maps and D-T1maps. 
Talabi et al (2008), and Arns and Melean (2009) simulated the 
magnetization decays of single phase fluid in carbonate rocks 
by the random walk method, and studied the characteristics 
of water and oil in T2 distributions. Talabi et al (2009, 2010) 
simulated the magnetization decays of single phase and two 
phase fluids in sandstone by the random walk method. Arns 
et  al (2011), and Alghamdi and Arns (2012) simulated the 
magnetization decays of the pulsed field gradient stimulated 
echo (PFGSTE) encoding sequence in Bentheimer sandstone 
and Berea sandstone, and studied the characteristics of water 
and oil in D-T2 maps. 
However, few studies have been done on NMR response 
simulation in tight sandstone, especially multi-dimensional 
NMR response. In this paper, we simulated NMR measure-
ments of tight sandstones by the random walk method based 
on digital core, and studied multi-dimensional NMR response 
of tight sandstone saturated with water and oil.
2. NMR theory
The basis of NMR is the interaction between the magnetic 
field of the nucleus and the external magnetic field. Before 
NMR measurements, the hydrogen protons of the formation 
fluid are orientated randomly and arranged arbitrarily; the 
spin system has no magnetization macroscopically in this 
case. Once measurement begins, the permanent magnet of the 
NMR logging tool produces a static magnetic field B0 which 
can make the disordered hydrogen protons of the formation 
fluid magnetized and in order in the nuclear spin space. The 
magnetization gradually reaches the equilibrium value M0, 
which has the same direction as B0.
The antenna of NMR logging tools produces an alternating 
magnetic field B1, the direction of which is perpendicular to 
that of B0. When the frequency (ω) of the alternating magnetic 
field is equal to the Larmor frequency (ω0) of protons, the 
resonance phenomenon will occur in the nuclear spin system; 
that is, the nuclear magnetic moment in the low energy state 
will jump to a high energy state by absorbing energy provided 
by the alternating magnetic field. Macroscopically, resonance 
leads to M0 deviating from B0, and M0 rotates 9 0° to the 
xy-plane by adjusting the pulse width of the alternating magn-
etic field. After the alternating magnetic field is removed, only 
the external magnetic field B0 in the z-axis direction persists, 
and M0 precesses along B0. The nuclear spin system will 
recover from the non-equilibrium state to equilibrium state. 
Magnetization M0 is decomposed into two components on the 
xy-plane (transverse component Mx and My) and one comp-
onent in the z-axis (longitudinal component Mz). The process 
of xy-plane components decay back to the initial zero state is 
defined as the transverse relaxation process, and the time con-
sumed is expressed as the transverse relaxation time T2. The 
process of z-axis component recovery back to the initial mag-
netization M0 is defined as the longitudinal relaxation process, 
and the time consumed is expressed as the longitudinal relaxa-
tion time T1. Bloch (19 4 6) described the changing of Mx, My, 
Mz with time in the rotating frame by

4. NMR response of tight sandstone
4.1 . Models of the digital core
The digital core technology has been widely used in studying 
petrophysical numerical simulations in porous media in which 
constructing an accurate digital core is its basis and key. At 
present, the method for constructing a digital core mainly 
includes the numerical reconstruction method and physical 
experimental method (Arns 2002, Jin et al 2003). The numer-
ical reconstruction method reconstructs the 3D digital core 
mainly based on the statistical function of 2D thin sections, 
but cannot guarantee the connectivity of the digital core.

4.4. NMR maps of tight sandstone sensitivity to SNR
The fluid signal in tight sandstone detected by NMR logging 
is very weak, because of low porosity. The SNR of downhole 
NMR data is low in tight sandstone reservoirs, and data SNR 
strongly affects the result of NMR data inversion. The echo 
trains of tight sandstone saturated with 50% wetting water and 
50% non-wetting oil obtained in section 4 .2 are used as an 
example. Gaussian noise of different SNR (SNR = 50, 20, 10) 
is added to echo trains data, and D-T2-T1 maps of tight sand-
stone with different SNR are obtained by the multiple echo 
trains jointing inversion method. The D-T2-T1 3D maps are 
projected onto D-T2, D-T1 and T1-T2 2D maps, respectively, 
as shown in figure 5. The meanings of various lines in figure 5 
are the same as those in figure 3.
As seen in figure 5, we can identify water and oil sig-
nals clearly in D-T2, D-T1 and T1-T2 maps, when SNR is 50. 
With decreasing SNR, the signals of water and oil gradually 
overlap one another and appear ‘long tail’ along the direction 
of decreasing diffusion coefficient in D-T2 and D-T1 maps, so 
the ability of identifying water and oil signals based on NMR 
maps gradually decreases at this time. The effects of SNR on 
T1-T2 maps is a little weaker than the effects of SNR on D-T2 
and D-T1 maps.
5. Restricted diffusion model
In porous media, fluid molecular motion is affected by the 
presence of pore walls, and diffusion is restricted such that the 
apparent diffusion coefficient is time dependent and smaller 
than that of the bulk fluid (Mitra et al 19 9 2, Latour et al 19 9 3, 
Hurlimann et  al 2002a, Luo et  al 2015). According to the 
D-T2-T1 maps of tight sandstone in section 4, restricted dif-
fusion has a great influence on the NMR response of wetting 
fluid. For the water-wet case, the water signal moves away 
from the water line, and for the oil-wet case, the oil signal 
moves away from the oil line in 2D diffusion-relaxation maps, 
which makes it hard to identify water and oil. So water could 
be mistaken for oil in the water-wet case and oil could be mis-
taken for water or oil of higher viscosity in the oil-wet case. 
We should consider the effects of restricted diffusion when 
interpreting D-T2-T1 maps of tight sandstone, especially in the 
small wetting fluid saturation case. The unrestricted diffusion

6. Conclusions
In this paper, magnetization decays of CPMG pulse sequences 
with different waiting times (TW ) and echo spacings (TE ) in 
tight sandstone were simulated, and D-T2-T1 maps of tight 
sandstone under different fluid saturation were obtained by 
the multiple echo trains jointing inversion method. Various 
fluids saturations, wettability, data SNR and restricted dif-
fusion effects on the NMR response of tight sandstone were 
thoroughly studied.
