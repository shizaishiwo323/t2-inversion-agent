Numerical simulation of (T2, T1) 2D NMR and ﬂ uid 
responses*
APPLIED GEOPHYSICS, Vol.9, No.4 (December 2012), P. 401 - 413, 8 Figures.
DOI:10.1007/s11770-012-0351-3
Tan Mao-Jin1,2, Zou You-Long2, Zhang Jin-Yan3, and Zhao Xin3
Abstract: One-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance (1D NMR) logging technology 
is limited for fluid typing, while two-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance (2D NMR) 
logging can provide more parameters including longitudinal relaxation time ( T1) and 
transverse relaxation time (T2) relative to fluid types in porous media. Based on the 2D 
NMR relaxation mechanism in a gradient magnetic field, echo train simulation and 2D 
NMR inversion are discussed in detail. For 2D NMR inversion, a hybrid inversion method 
is proposed based on the damping least squares method (LSQR) and an improved truncated 
singular value decomposition (TSVD) algorithm. A series of spin echoes are ﬁ rst simulated 
with multiple waiting times (TWs) in a gradient magnetic field for given fluid models and 
these synthesized echo trains are inverted by the hybrid method. The inversion results are 
consistent with given models. Moreover, the numerical simulation of various ﬂ uid models 
such as the gas-water, light oil-water, and vicious oil-water models were carried out with 
different echo spacings (TEs) and TWs by this hybrid method. Finally, the influences of 
different signal-to-noise ratios (SNRs) on inversion results in various fluid models are 
studied. The numerical simulations show that the hybrid method and optimized observation 
parameters are applicable to ﬂ uid typing of gas-water and oil-water models.
Keywords: Two-dimensional NMR (2D-NMR) logging, hybrid inversion method, transverse 
relaxation time (T2), longitudinal relaxation time (T1), ﬂ uid typing
Introduction
Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) logging is a 
unique logging method to distinguish movable and bound 
fluids in formations and is widely used in hydrocarbon 
identification (Coats et al., 1998; Xiao, 1998; Wang et 
al., 2001; Xiao, 2007). However, the 1D NMR logging 
technology can only measure transverse relaxation 
time (T2) of fluids in pores, so it is greatly limited in 
identification and quantitative evaluation of fluids such 
as oil, gas, and water in some reservoirs. That is because 
when oil, gas, and water coexist in local pores their 
signals sometimes overlap each other in the T2 spectrum. 
To distinguish them from each other, the usual differential 
spectrum method (DSM), shifted spectrum method (SSM), 
and enhanced diffusion method (EDM) are difﬁ cult (Wang 
et al., 2001; Shao, 2003; Tan et al., 2006).

Two-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance (2D 
NMR) logging is newly developed and can provide a 
variety of parameters including transverse relaxation 
time (T2), observed longitudinal relaxation time (T1), 
fluid diffusion coefficient (D), internal magnetic field 
gradient (G), and other parameters. The current 1D 
NMR logging cannot meet 2D observation requirements, 
so it has to redesign its observation activations and 
corresponding parameters. The new NMR instruments 
such as MREx of Baker Atlas and MRX of Schlumberger 
can both be used for 2D NMR logging. 
Hurlimann et al. (2002) and Sun and Dunn (2005a; 
2005b) proposed a method to improve spin-echo pulse 
sequence (CPMG) using two windows and achieved 
2D NMR logging based on relaxation- diffusion ( T2, 
D) analysis, namely (T2, D) 2D NMR, which greatly 
enhanced the accuracy of fluid identification and 
saturation calculations. Xiao (1998, 2007) and Xie 
et al. (2005, 2009) have performed some 2D NMR 
experiments with man-made paramagnetic sandstone and 
water-saturated natural shale sandstone and have given 
two-dimensional distributions of transverse relaxation 
time (T2) and internal magnetic field gradient (G) 
namely (T2, G) 2D NMR, which is signiﬁ cant for NMR 
measurement analysis of complex terrestrial sedimentary 
rock strata. Gu and Liu (2007) and Gu et al. (2009) have 
studied traditional singular value decomposition (SVD), 
improved the SVD algorithm, and have achieved a two-
dimensional continuous spectrum for NMR inversion 
and discussed the impacts of signal-noise-ratio (SNR) 
on inversion results. Tan et al. (2011) used the truncated 
singular value decomposition (TSVD) for inversion of 
(T2, D) 2D NMR. The experiment was performed with 
different magnetic field gradients with different echo 
spacing groups using gas-water models with different 
gas saturation and the response characteristics of the 
different saturation models were analyzed. Furthermore, 
the effects of different SNRs on inversion results were 
studied. 
In this paper, we introduce and study the basic 
principles and mathematical model of 2D NMR logging 
with multiple waiting times (Tw) based on the NMR 
relaxation mechanism under conditions of the gradient 
magnetic field. Then, the LSQR and TSVD algorithms 
are used to realize ( T2, T1) 2D NMR inversion. 
Combined with the characteristics of the two algorithms, 
a hybrid inversion method for 2D NMR inversion is 
proposed using the LSQR first and then the TSVD 
algorithm is used for a second inversion. Whose initial 
guess is from the LSQR result. In the experiment, the 
hybrid inversion is better than a single algorithm. Using 
this new inversion method, the 2D spectrum inversion of 
different ﬂ uid models are implemented and the inﬂ uence 
of different combinations of multiple Tw and different 
TE are studied in detail. The inﬂ uences of different echo 
spacing, different signal to noise ratio (SNR) on the 
inversion results are studied. All the research results 
provide an important guidance for the parameter design 
for 2D NMR logging.

The LSQR method was proposed by Paige and 
Sanders in 1982 and is used for solving least squares 
problems with the Lanczos iteration method. The LSQR 
method has the advantage of simplified sparse matrix 
calculations and it is suitable for solving large sparse 
matrix problems (Liu and Liu, 2006; Yang et al., 2008).
The improved truncated singular value decomposition 
(TSVD) algorithm is also used to solve most of the 
linear least squares problems. The TSVD method can 
give an optimal answer but loses the most powerful 
oscillation component of the solution, so the method 
reduces the results resolution which makes the spectrum 
discontinuous. In order to obtain a continuous spectrum, 
the solution is pointed to the minimum of ||A∆x–∆b||2 to 
solve for the optimal solution. First, assuming a known 
initial guess x0, the original equation (2) is rewritten as 
A(x – x0) = b – b0 or A∆x = ∆b, which is executed by the 
TSVD algorithm. When ||Ax–b||2 is a minimum, ∆
x is the optimal solution, so x0 + ∆x is the ﬁ nal result of 
equation (2). Since the equation solution of A∆x = ∆b is 
∆x, it does not reduce the matrix size in the non-negative 
constraints. If components of the solution are less than 
zero, these components are directly changed to zero 
and then the changed solutions are reiterated until the 
components meet the non-negative conditions. Because 
the singular value decomposition process of matrix Am×n 
is executed only once, the amount of computations and 
computing time are greatly reduced. 
To verify the hybrid inversion method, a gas reservoir 
model containing bound water and free gas is built 
and the fluid properties are listed in Table 1. In the 
simulation experiment, the two-dimensional grid mesh 
with transverse relaxation time (T2) and longitudinal 
relaxation time (T1) is a 30 × 30 logarithmic uniform 
distribution (Tan et al., 2008). Fluid composition in the 
two-dimensional spectrum is a Gaussian distribution. The 
two-dimensional spectrum with multiple peaks is shown 
in Figure 1a. The fluid model observation parameters 
are: echo spacing (TE) is 1.2 ms and the group of waiting 
times (Tw) is set to be 0.2s, 0.8s, 2.0s, 4.0s, 8.0s, and 
12.0s. The number of echoes of each received echo train 
is 600. The magnetic ﬁ eld gradient is 2.0 × 10-3 T/cm and 
six groups of echo trains are simulated (see Figure 1b). 
In this case, the condition number of matrix A3600×900 is 
calculated to be about 4.0 × 1025, which indicates that 
it is a serious pathological matrix. The LSQR and SVD 
algorithms are combined to solve the two-dimensional 
inversion problem.

Similarly, Figure 2b shows two-dimensional inversion 
results of the TSVD algorithm with non-negative 
constraints. The two-dimensional spectrum looks like 
the given model in Figure 1a. The long one-dimensional 
T2 distribution (>100 ms) relaxation components and 
short T1 relaxation components of the one-dimensional 
T1 distribution (<100 ms) match well with those of the 
given model in one-dimensional T1 and T2 distributions 
but the short T2 components between 1 and 100 ms and 
long T1 components between 100 and 3000 ms do not 
match with the given model. Comparisons of the two 
inversion algorithms indicate that the LSQR has faster 
inversion speed but lower accuracy. TSVD inversion 
accuracy can satisfy the requirements but the speed is 
relatively slow. Moreover, the TSVD algorithm solution 
is not complete due to decreasing the solution dimension 
and the lost components should be compensated for to 
improve the resolution and accuracy. From Figures 2a 
and 2b, the LSQR method for inversion of the short T2 
components is good, whereas the long T2 components 
inverted by the improved TSVD method are better for 
inversion of long T2 components. Therefore, a hybrid 
method with TSVD and LSQR algorithms is considered 
to elevate the inversion accuracy. That is, the inversion 
is ﬁ rst conducted by LSQR and then the results are set as 
initial values x0 and then the TSVD inversion algorithm 
is used for further inversion.
Figure 2c shows the inversion results by the hybrid 
method, from which we see that the inversion results 
are greatly improved. The T2 inverted distribution is 
consistent with the model in short relaxation components 
as well as long components and the inverted T1 
distribution is also closer to the model. In the hybrid 
inversion process, the number of iterations for the LSQR 
method is about 800 and for the TSVD algorithm is 400 
and the total computing time is 1722.41 s with inversion 
error of 0.07. Therefore, this hybrid method is more 
accurate and faster than a single LSQR or SVD inversion 
algorithm. 
Observation parameter design and 
analysis
Observation parameter design is essential for 
fluid identification by nuclear magnetic resonance 
measurements. The data core matrix ( Am×n) and the 
observed echo train (bm) are not only related to the 
transverse and longitudinal relaxation time but also 
are affected by different combinations of waiting time 
and echo spacing (TE). The influences of observation 
parameters on inversion results should be studied 
further. Therefore, the oil/water and gas/water models 
are designed for experimental study of inversion for 
distinguishing oil or gas from water. The ﬂ uid parameters 
are listed in Tables 2 and 3 and the corresponding 
models are shown in Figure 3.

4, we see that the echo spacing has almost no impact on 
the accuracy of the inversion for the oil-water model.

Different waiting time (Tw) groups
For studying the influences of waiting times (Tw) on 
the inversion results, different waiting time groups are 
set for a series of simulation and inversion experiments. 
The observation parameters are: the echo spacing is 
0.6 ms, the number of echoes is 300 for each echo train 
corresponding to each waiting time. The waiting time 
groups are set to 0.05 s, 0.1 s, 1.0 s, 1.5 s, 3.0 s, 5.6 s, 6.0 
s, 6.5 s, 7.0 s, 8.0 s, 10.0 s, and 12.0 s and 0.05 s, 0.1 s, 1.0 
s, 3.0 s, 8.0 s, and 12.0 s. First, six groups of echo trains 
are simulated and then the simulation data is inverted 
using the hybrid inversion method. The inversion results 
of different ﬂ uid models are illustrated in Figures 6 and 7.
When the waiting time group is chosen as 0.05 s, 0.1 
s, 1.0 s, 1.5 s, 3.0 s, and 5.6s, the relative error (Rx) is 
about 0.1877. When waiting time group is 6.0 s, 6.5 s, 
7.0 s, 8.0 s, 10.0 s, and 12.0s, the relative error (Rx) is 
about 0.7559. When the waiting time group is 0.05 s, 
0.1 s, 1.0 s, 3.0 s, 8.0 s, and 12.0 s, the relative error 
(Rx) is about 0.2031. From 2D spectrum inversion and 
error analysis, the results with the waiting time group of 
0.05 s, 0.1 s, 1.0 s, 3.0 s, 8.0 s, and 12.0s is best in three 
tests in which every ﬂ uid peak is focused well and the 
T2 and T1 distributions also match well with the model. 
Therefore, in multiple Tw observation modes, for the oil-
water model, the inversion results of the mixed waiting 
time group with short and long Tw not only reflect the 
bound water with short T2 relaxation time and short T1 
relaxation time but can also identify the components 
with long T2 and T1 relaxation times in porous media. 
From two-dimensional inversion spectra, light oil 
and viscous oil also easily can be distinguished from 
movable water and bound water.

The experiments were also performed for the oil-water 
models. The experiment results show that when the SNR 
ratio is 150, heavy oil can be distinguished from bound 
water in the viscous oil and water model but the movable 
water and light oil overlap each other in the light oil-
water model, so they are difﬁ cult to be identiﬁ ed. When 
the SNR is 100, the viscous oil and bound water can still 
be distinguished from each other in the heavy oil-water 
model. However, the movable water and light oil overlap 
and light oil is not distinguished from movable water 
in the light oil and water model. When the SNR ratio 
is lower than 50, viscous oil and bound water overlap 
and movable water and light oil also overlap. From the 
experiments, we conclude that the SNR ratio effect on 
inversion results in the gas-water model is lower than in 
the oil-water model for ﬂ uid identiﬁ cation.
Conclusions
The proposed non-negative LSQR and SVD hybrid 
inversion method achieves (T2, T1) 2D NMR inversion 
with multiple-Tw activation. The hybrid method can 
be used through redesigned acquisition parameters. 
Numerical simulation experiments show that the hybrid 
inversion method can meet the needs of practical 
application. From the inversion results and error 
analysis, we conclude that: 
(1) The hybrid inversion method is stable and more 
accurate than a single inversion algorithm which 
decomposes a matrix only once. It shows fast calculation 
speed and only needs small storage space. The algorithm 
easily achieves the non-negative constraints and can get 
a continuous 2D high-precision spectrum.
(2) With respect to the acquisition parameters, the 
mixed waiting time combination with short and long 
waiting times are more suitable for ﬂ uid identiﬁ cation. 
The results identify bound ﬂ uids with the fast relaxation 
components and oil or gas and water with the slow 
relaxation component. 
(3) The SNR ratio effect on inversion results in the 
gas-water model is lower than in the oil-water model for 
ﬂ uid identiﬁ cation.
(4) The reservoir fluids can be qualitatively 
determined by the location and amplitude of the focused 
signals in the two-dimensional spectrum. For light 
oil-water and gas-water models, both oil and gas are 
distinguished from water more easily, whereas for ﬂ uid 
models containing viscous oil, the oil and water are not 
easily identiﬁ ed because the NMR relaxation properties 
of bound ﬂ uids and viscous oil are very similar.
