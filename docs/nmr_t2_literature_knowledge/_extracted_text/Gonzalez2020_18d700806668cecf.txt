Simulations of NMR Relaxation in a Real Porous Structure: 
Pre‑asymptotic Behavior to the Localization Regime
J. L. Gonzalez1  · E. L. de Faria2 · Marcelo P . Albuquerque2 · 
Marcio P . Albuquerque2 · Clécio R. Bom3 · J. C. C. Freitas1 · Maury D. Correia4
Received: 5 October 2019 / Revised: 13 April 2020 / Published online: 5 May 2020

Abstract
The knowledge of the different physical mechanisms determining the relaxation of 
the magnetization is crucial to obtain reliable petrophysical parameters from NMR 
experiments. Some studies have focused on asymptotic mechanisms providing sim-
ple expressions for the relaxation rate in terms of parameters of the system. The 
main difficulties in solving this problem are related to the existence of intrinsic mag-
netic inhomogeneities, in addition to the complex structure of the porous geometry 
found in real systems. In this work, simulations of the relaxation of the transverse 
magnetization were performed on a real pore structure obtained from X-ray microto-
mography images. Also, a statistical method was used to introduce a local magnetic 
non-homogeneity, resembling local field distributions discussed in several works 
in the literature. The conditions of our simulations allowed the study of relaxation 
processes mainly influenced by local magnetic gradients, while surface relaxation 
became irrelevant. The dependence of the relaxation rate on the inter-echo spac-
ing was studied, where for low spacings, the results reproduced those expected in 
the free-diffusion limit. On the other hand, for high inter-echo spacings, the relaxa-
tion rate deviates from the expected dependence, indicating the emergence of a 
new relaxation mechanism. This high-spacing behavior was associated with a pre-
asymptotic localization regime. Our data suggest a logarithmic dependence of the 
relaxation rate with the inter-echo spacing in this transition regime.

1 Introduction
Porous rocks are basically heterogeneous systems which are the basic components 
of oil reservoirs, mineral deposits, soils, and so on [1]. Nuclear magnetic resonance 
(NMR) is a powerful tool commonly used in the study of the geometry and physi-
cal–chemical properties associated with the porous space within natural rocks. The 
technique in essence explores the fact that the surface of the pores and the magnetic 
properties of the system modify the relaxation rate and the diffusion properties of 
the fluids within the pores. Studies with NMR provide information on the pore-size 
distributions, surface to volume ratio of the pores, pore-throat distributions, fluid 
typing, etc. [2, 3]. This information is often deduced from 
T2 distributions (describ-
ing the relaxation of the transverse magnetization), usually determined through 
CPMG experiments [3, 4]. The characteristic time of the CPMG sequence is the 
inter-echo spacing 
tE between two adjacent echoes [3, 4].
The interpretation of CPMG results depends on the comprehension of theoretical 
models describing the physics behind T2 relaxation experiments. It is well estab-
lished that for fluids within rock pores, there exist three independent physical mech-
anisms involved in the 
M xy relaxation. The bulk relaxation related to fluid interac-
tions ( T2B ), a surface relaxation that takes into account the interactions of the fluid 
with the pore surfaces ( T2S ), and a third one which considers the diffusion process in 
the presence of magnetic field gradients ( T2diﬀ ). In principle, these three mecha-
nisms act in parallel leading to the following expression for the relaxation rate

11, 14]. On the other hand, when lG ≪ (lP, lD ) (i.e., for large magnetic field varia-
tions) the ensemble of spins loses the phase coherence during diffusion, and this 
decoherence introduces a significant attenuation of the transverse magnetization. 
For pores under this condition, the magnetization is not uniform in the whole 
porous space. This asymptotic behavior is known as localization regime [8 –11].
Often pore-size distributions are found through CPMG experiments by trans-
forming relaxation time distributions into pore size distributions, assuming that 
the dominant mechanism is the surface relaxation one. Depending on the experi-
ment, this procedure can be questionable since sedimentary rocks show an intrin-
sic distribution of internal magnetic fields within the porous space of rocks. This 
local magnetic inhomogeneity is basically due to the contrast between the mag-
netic susceptibilities of the fluid and the solid matrix [8 , 10], in addition to the 
possible existence of magnetic impurities.
In this sense, the interpretation of magnetic relaxation due to molecular diffu-
sion in the presence of local non-uniform magnetic fields is a very complicated 
problem. The principal difficulty in solving this issue is that a realistic descrip-
tion of the variations of the magnetic field within natural rocks is not straight-
forward as to be described by a simple phenomenological model. Note that two 
samples can have similar average bulk susceptibilities (i.e., similar phases with 
equal volume proportions), still showing different gradient distributions due to 
different spatial distribution of the phases. In other words, local distributions of 
solid phases within a rock can vary depending of their localization within the 
rock; therefore, estimating these field distributions for the whole rock by simply 
observing a local information seems to be a huge challenge. In principle, know -
ing local fields implies to solve the Maxwell equations at the pore level within the 
rock by considering spatial dependent susceptibilities [15]. Thus, any approach 
to this problem can help understand the physics behind the 
M xy relaxation in the 
presence of internal magnetic gradients.
Different studies in the last years have focused on the field distribution within 
natural rocks and its influence on the transverse magnetization relaxation [8–11, 
14–16]. In Ref. [15], internal field (and gradient) distributions were simulated based 
on 2D-section of a sedimentary rock. The gradient distributions were single peak 
with an average value around zero. These results evidenced the internal field is pro-
portional to the applied external field and the susceptibility difference in the porous 
media, being bounded inside a field interval. Also an embracing attempt to model 
magnetic field heterogeneity at the pore space can be observed in [16]. The authors 
solved Maxwell equations on meshes obtained directly from digital rocks using vol-
ume averaged magnetic susceptibilities measured at magnetic fields usually explored 
in NMR experiments. Their results captured some of the principal features observed 
in magnetic gradient measurements, but the simulations did fail in observing large 
gradients experimentally measured in rocks. All these results share common fea-
tures, for example, the local fields are bounded by extreme values which are related 
to the susceptibilities of the porous media, while at the same time evidence the dif-
ficulty of the studied problem. So, as stated in [9], the absence of theoretical mod-
els capable of reproducing intrinsic field variations at large scale can be eventually 
avoided through the use of simulations or heuristic models.

Strong gradients (whatever be the cause) can lead to the emergence of the locali-
zation regime (i.e., lG ≪ (lP, lD ) ), where the main mechanism governing the relaxa-
tion of the magnetization is the strong dephasing between spins. The localization 
regime occurs at magnetic gradients around tens of miliTesla per meter, which can 
be easily obtained in scanners used in materials science and other areas [17]. Previ-
ous studies suggest this regime is a generic feature of diffusive NMR [9, 17–19], 
with evidences of a stretched-exponential decay of the overall magnetization for 
well-defined pore geometries, such as slabs and cylindrical pores [19]. The analysis 
of this asymptotic regime in natural porous rocks represents a huge challenge due 
to the complex structure of the pore space in these systems. Thus, theoretical and 
experimental studies of the localization regime are very important to interpret NMR 
data in the presence of strong magnetic gradients.
In this study, we have simulated the emergence of the localization regime on a 
real pore structure obtained from X-ray microtomography images of a sedimentary 
rock. In essence, CPMG experiments were simulated on a digital rock containing 
a distribution of internal magnetic gradients. The novelty of our study lies in the 
introduction of a real pore space as a suitable geometry to explore the localization 
regime in CPMG experiments. We have also developed a new and simple method 
that allows the introduction of a local field distribution inside the pore space. This 
method is new and it incorporates some relevant physics associated with the pres-
ence of internal fields inside porous geometries, as reported in the literature (see, 
for example, reference [15]). Thus, in our simulations, local field distributions can 
be used as control parameter to identify correlations between local magnetic field 
gradients (microscopic approach) and macroscopic parameters that can be experi-
mentally determined in NMR experiments.
2 Results and Discussion
The digital rock was obtained from /u1D707 CT images obtained on a sedimentary rock for-
mation located in Brazil Northeast region, called Lagoa  Salgada. The used scanner 
was a GE phoenix vjtomejx L 300 equipment, and the resolution of the images was 
17.2 
/u1D707m/pixels. The images were pre-processed with Matlab techniques and later 
segmented through a k-means algorithm. This process provided a pore structure

important to stress that the image processing workflow used in this work was aimed 
to obtain a somewhat realistic pore representation of the rock to be used in our simu-
lations. Thus, we were not concerned with small differences between the obtained 
pore representation and the real porous geometry within the rock since the unique 
goal was to afford a realistic pore representation resembling the actual porous struc-
ture found within natural rocks. Details regarding the segmentation process used in 
this work can be found in Ref. [20], while rigorous processing workflows of 
/u1D707 CT 
images are discussed in [21].

Another interesting point regarding NMR experiments is that molecular diffusion 
in a rock occurs inside pores and also between pores (interpore coupling). Usually, 
theoretical considerations are developed by considering the weak coupling regime, 
where the interpore diffusion is neglected while the overall decay in the transverse 
magnetization is controlled by relaxation processes simultaneously occurring in iso-
lated pores with different sizes. Even if this scenario might not be strictly applicable 
to natural rocks, it has been often used to extract information in NMR experiments 
[31]. In our study, the segmentation of the 
/u1D707 CT images provided a digital rock con-
taining an ensemble of isolated pores (weak coupling regime). Each isolated pore 
was formed by a particular group of interconnected voxels, where each voxel shares 
one face with at least another voxel of the same pore. Thus, our NMR simulations 
reproduced the relaxation of the magnetization under the weak coupling regime.

Regarding the high-tE regime shown in Fig. 3, it is to be stressed that as tE increases 
lD turns to be larger than lG , but still significantly lower than lS . This condition indi-
cates that a new regime is dominating the M xy decay, specifically the localization limit 
(non-homogeneous magnetization in the pores). Nevertheless, in this asymptotic limit 
theoretical models indicate T2 does not depend on the inter-echo spacing, which does 
not agree with the results shown in Fig.  3. Thus, we suggest that the second linear 
regime observed in Fig. 3 for higher tE values corresponds to a transition between two 
asymptotic limits. This idea is not new; in fact, it has been previously suggested, where 
the existence of a preasymptotic localization regime with irrelevant surface relaxation 
could lead to some dependence of the relaxation rate on the inter-echo spacing [9]. As 
stated in the same work [9], no theoretical model has been developed to describe this 
physical situation.

tE ≥ tEc can be 
associated with the non-homogeneity of bounded magnetic fields within the sample. 
This means that whichever the position within the porous space, there exists a different 
local gradient affecting the spins that diffuse in this region. From a physical viewpoint, 
this idea means that the temporal dependence of 
M xy should be controlled by the local 
details of the magnetic structure of the rock (i.e., averaged local gradients). Therefore, 
at

remaining spins proceed to relax under the free-diffusion limit. In other words, the sig-
nal is formed by isolated pockets of surviving magnetization [18]. As 
tE increases, a 
higher proportion of spins diffuse in regions where they loss completely their coher-
ence (i.e., lG < lD ), while other spins remain in pockets with small effective gradients. 
These spin bundles contribute to the dependence between the relaxation rate and tE

It should be highlighted that the analysis of NMR data in the framework of the 
localization regime can be crucial to determine accurate petrophysical properties of 
reservoir rocks [18]. Our simulations clearly indicate that 
T2 distributions can be sig-

magnetic susceptibilities, parameters of the pore structure commonly determined 
from 
T2 distributions (such as pore-size distributions and surface to volume ratios, 
for example) can be misinterpreted if these gradients are not properly considered. 
This can induce to wrong results for the estimation of macroscopic parameters, for 
example, porosity or permeability [18]. Concerning this point, an appropriate analy-
sis of 
T2 distributions, considering the physics behind the localization regime, can 
shed some light on the magnetic structure of porous rocks. In this sense, the analysis 
of statistical features of 
T2 distributions (logarithmic mean, modal value, etc.), in 
addition to other experimental parameters (for example, tEc in Fig.  5) can help to 
quantify internal gradients and consequently magnetic susceptibilities. Ultimately, 
this can be an indicative of the possible existence of magnetic phases inside the 
rocks, which can be very useful to classify natural rocks.

3 Conclusions
In summary, we have simulated the transverse magnetization relaxation in a real 
porous structure obtained from X-ray microtomography images of a sedimentary 
rock. The simulations included a local distribution of magnetic fields within the 
porous geometry. The field distribution was simulated through a statistical approach, 
which introduced some properties commonly reported in other studies related to the 
presence of magnetic non-homogeneities in natural rocks. Still, the simulations were 
conducted under particular conditions assuming that the relaxation of the transverse 
magnetization was mainly associated with diffusion processes occurring in the pres-
ence of strong magnetic gradients. Our results show a striking influence of the mag-
netic gradient distribution on the relaxation rate (characterized by the logarithmic 
mean of the 
T2 distribution). The relaxation rate depends linearly on the square of 
the inter-echo spacing at low values (free-diffusion regime), while this dependence 
turns to be somewhat logarithmic for high 
tE values. This high-tE regime was associ-
ated with a pre-asymptotic localization regime previously suggested in the literature. 
Lastly, a phenomenological model was used to fit the data in all range of 
tE values 
simulated in this work. Our work points out to the importance of exploring this pre-
asymptotic relaxation regime, which can be experimentally investigated in future 
work.
