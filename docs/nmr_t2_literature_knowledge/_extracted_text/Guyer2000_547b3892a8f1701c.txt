The magnetic moments of ﬂuid particles ﬁlling the pore space of a porous material ~1! reside in a complex
space, ~2! are carried by the particles in diffusive exploration of the pore space, and ~3! relax when the particles
approach relaxation sites on the walls of the pore space. Further, when the magnetic moments are the object of
a nuclear magnetic resonance experiment, they are manipulated by rf magnetic ﬁelds, internal magnetic ﬁeld
gradients, and applied magnetic ﬁeld gradient pulses. In this paper, a lattice Boltzmann computational proce-
dure is described that accounts for all of the vagaries in the experience of a magnetic moment in a porous
material. The time evolution of the longitudinal and transverse magnetization, in a variety of experimental
situations, can be simulated with this computational procedure. The z component of the magnetization, the
longitudinal magnetization, is described by a set of coarse grained distribution functions for a scalar ﬂuid. The
time evolution of these distribution functions involves a scattering process ~to account for diffusion ! and a
probability of transmission out of the pore space ~to account for surface relaxation !. A numerical example,
involving a pore adjacent to a microporous region, is examined in detail. The transverse magnetization is a
vector. Its x and y magnetization components are carried by separate scalar ﬂuids. There is a set of coarse
grained distribution functions for each ﬂuid. Radio frequency magnetic ﬁelds, internal magnetic ﬁeld gradients,
applied magnetic ﬁeld gradient pulses, etc., represent conversion processes in which the two ﬂuids transform
into one another. Two examples, one involving a periodic ﬁeld gradient and a Hahn echo, and the other
involving an isolated pore and a PFG sequence, are examined in detail.
I. INTRODUCTION
The time evolution of the magnetization of a ﬂuid M(t)i n
a pore space is in many instances controlled by the geometry
of the pore space. Thus nuclear magnetic resonance ~NMR!
measurements are one of the principle ways to learn about
pore space geometry.
1,2 Sophisticated NMR tools, 1 and
elaborate experimental protocols 3,4 are an essential part of
current practice and development.
The description of M(t) of a ﬂuid in a pore space has four
main ingredients. ~1! The ﬂuid resides in a complicated pore
space. ~2! The magnetic moments carried by the ﬂuid mol-
ecules diffusively explore this pore space. ~3! The magnetic
moments relax when they are carried close to relaxation sites
on the walls of the pore space. ~4! The time evolution of the
magnetization is measured using NMR ﬁelds that label, ma-
nipulate, and interrogate the magnetic moments. NMR ﬁelds
include radio frequency ~rf! pulses, gradients, and applied
gradient pulses. In some instances these ﬁelds simply set the
initial conditions, as in the case of a T
1 measurement. In
other instances these ﬁelds modify the magnetic system im-
portantly as the time evolution is occuring.
Given M(t), a magnetization relaxation curve, what is the
pore space geometry? This inverse problem is solved by for-
ward calculations using a sequence of models for the behav-
ior of the magnetization in a pore space. The success of
solution depends critically on the quality and practicality of
the models. Perhaps the best known model of magnetization
relaxation in a pore space is the isolated pore model of
Brownstein and Tarr.
5 This model is solved for simple ge-
ometries in terms of the relaxation modes of the magnetiza-
tion. The solution to this model in the fast diffusion limit is
used to solve the inverse problem in which a spectrum of
relaxation times is mapped onto a spectrum of pore radii.
6
The isolated pore model is often used to assess the impor-
tance and interplay of physical variables. 7,8 Models of mag-
netization relaxation in more complex geometries have been
discussed in a number of approximations, e.g., taking the
pore space to be periodic,
9 or assuming the behavior of the
magnetization in each pore is described by a single relax-
ation mode in order to study pore-pore coupling. 8,10,11 These
models provide an understanding of average properties and
limiting behavior, but are relatively inﬂexible. Models of
magnetization relaxation in complex geometries have been
solved exactly using random walk methods.
12,13 Random
walk methods are used to conﬁrm approximate solutions and
to provide insight in complicated situations. They are too
computationally intensive to be used for data analysis.
Lattice Boltzmann methods have been very useful in
‘‘solving’’ a wide variety of difﬁcult transport problems.
14,15
This is because one is able to give up details about the short
range behavior of a ﬂuid in return for having complex inter-
actions in the ﬂuid or for having the ﬂuid in a complex space.
The problem at hand is ideally suited to this trade off. The
purpose of this paper is to describe in detail the use of lattice
Boltzmann methods for solution to problems involving mag-
netization motion in a pore space. One of the nice things
about the lattice Boltzmann computational procedure in this
context is that it lends itself naturally to description of the
labeling, manipulation, and interrogation of the ﬂuid that are

Section II contains a lattice Boltzmann description of the
time evolution of the z component of the magnetization. The
computational rules for specifying the pore space geometry,
the particle diffusion, and the magnetization relaxation at the
surface of the pore space are described. In Sec. III the result-
ing lattice Boltzmann computational procedure is applied to
the relaxation of the magnetization in a pore adjacent to a
microporous region of space. In Sec. IV the procedure for
handling the transverse magnetization is described. The
transverse magnetization is described by two scalar ﬂuids
and a conversion process between the two ﬂuids. One scalar
ﬂuid carries the x component of the magnetization, and a
second scalar ﬂuid carries the y component of the magneti-
zation. Each of these ﬂuids obeys the rules developed in Sec.
II. The conversion process between the two ﬂuids accounts
for precession of the transverse magnetization in local mag-
netic ﬁelds. The resulting lattice Boltzmann computational
procedure for the motion of transverse magnetization is ap-
plied to ~1! a Hahn echo for magnetization in a sinusoidal
gradient and ~2! a pulsed ﬁeld gradient ~PFG! probe of the
magnetization in a bounded pore space. Section V contains
our conclusions and suggestions for future work. Certain
useful details are found in two Appendixes.

V. CONCLUSION
We have developed a lattice Boltzmann procedure to nu-
merically simulate the time evolution of magnetization in a
porous medium. The essential ingredients of the simulations
are ~1! a complex pore space in which the magnetization
resides, ~2! diffusive exploration of the pore space by the
particles carrying the magnetization, ~3! relaxation of the
magnetization at the pore space-matrix interface, and ~4! ma-
nipulation of the magnetization by various NMR ﬁelds.
Our computational procedure employs a set of rules for
evolving the distribution functions appropriate to a coarse
grained picture of the particle system. Time is coarse grained
on scale
t, the pore space-matrix system ~porous medium! is
coarse grained on scale a, and velocity space is coarse
grained by contraction to six velocity vectors of equal mag-
nitude ~in two dimensions !.
The rules for the evolution of the distribution functions in
one time step involve translation in discrete steps of ampli-
tude a, a scattering process of strength r to account for par-
ticle diffusion, and a transmission coefﬁcient
x, operating at
the pore space-matrix interface, to account for surface relax-
ation. These computational rules can be reduced to hydrody-
namic equations in the k!0 and
v!0 limits, e.g., a con-
tinuum diffusion equation results from the Boltzmann limit.
The relationship between parameters in the computational
scheme, and parameters in the hydrodynamic equations is
established by conducting computational experiments.
The lattice Boltzmann procedure can be used to study the
time evolution of longitudinal magnetization in an arbitrarily
complex porous material. In this case, a single ﬂuid diffuses
in the pore space and relaxes ~disappears! due to interactions
at the pore walls. When the particles in the system carry an
internal degree of freedom, as they do in the case of trans-
verse magnetization, a two ﬂuid model is employed. Each
ﬂuid carries one of the components of the internal degree of
freedom. In the case of transverse magnetization one ﬂuid
carries the x magnetization ( x ﬂuid! and a second ﬂuid ( y
ﬂuid! carries the y magnetization. Field gradients and rf
ﬁelds are coupled conveniently to the internal degree of free-
dom in a lattice Boltzmann calculation. The precession of
transverse magnetization maps onto a conversion process be-
tween the x ﬂuid and the y ﬂuid that occurs pointwise
throughout the pore space. Consequently the inﬂuence of
complicated internal and external precession ﬁelds on the
time evolution of the transverse magnetization can be de-
scribed. Lattice Boltzmann methods are also well suited to
problems in which a ﬂow ﬁeld is present.
22
A real experiment on the time evolution of magnetization
is a very powerful tool. However, the results are necessarily
an average over mesoscopic to macroscopic length scales.
Much more detail can be probed in a computational experi-
ment. The real experiment is telling you about the behavior
of ﬂuid magnetization in a real material, e.g., a Berea sand-
stone. The computational experiment is telling you about the
behavior of ﬂuid magnetization in a model material. The
point of studying the model material is to look in great detail
at the magnetization evolution, and to glean an understand-
ing from what you see. This understanding may lead to better
models of the real experiments, suggest useful manipulations
of the NMR ﬁelds, suggest methods of data analysis, etc.
Thus, numerical studies of model materials complement ex-
perimental studies of real materials. The lattice Boltzmann
computational procedure is well suited to this task.
