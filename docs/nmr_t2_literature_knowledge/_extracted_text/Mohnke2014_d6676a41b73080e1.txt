Abstract Nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) relaxometry is a useful tool to estimate transport and stor-
age properties of rocks and soils. However, as there is no unique relation between the NMR signal and these
properties in rocks, a variety of empirical models on deriving hydraulic properties from NMR relaxometry
data have been published. Complementary to laboratory measurements, this paper introduces a numerical
framework to jointly simulate NMR relaxometry experiments and two-phase ﬂow on the micrometer scale.
Herein, the NMR diffusion equations were tied to an established Lattice Boltzmann algorithm used in com-
putational ﬂuid dynamics. The numerically simulated NMR data were validated for both surface-limited and
diffusion-limited relaxation regimes using analytical solutions available for fully and partially water-
saturated simple pore geometries. Subsequently, simulations were compiled using a complex pore space
derived from three-dimensional computer tomography (CT) data of an unconsolidated sand and the results
were compared to respective NMR T
1 relaxometry data. The NMR transients simulated for different water
saturations matched the measured data regarding initial amplitudes (i.e., porosity and saturation) and relax-
ation behavior (i.e., distribution of water-saturated pores). Thus, we provide a simulation tool that enables
study of the inﬂuences of structural and physicochemical properties, such as pore connectivity and pore
coupling, surface relaxivity, or diffusivity, on partially saturated porous media, e.g, rocks or soils, with NMR
T
1 relaxometry data.
1. Introduction
The application of nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) to petroleum and environmental research issues has
become more important in recent years owing to the method’s direct sensitivity to the hydrogen content in
pore ﬂuids such as oil, water, or methane gas. The NMR technique is a useful tool that allows inferences of
transport and storage parameters such as pore-size distributions or the relative permeability in a porous sys-
tem, such as rock formations or soils. Even though a considerable amount of data on deriving hydraulic
properties of porous media by NMR relaxometry have been published [e.g., Kenyon et al ., 1988; Sen et al .,
1990; review in Dunn et al ., 2002], the majority of these models only empirically address the fully water-
saturated state.
The aim of this study is therefore to further advance the fundamental understanding of the relationship
between NMR parameters and transport and storage properties of fully and partially saturated pore systems
in porous media. To achieve this goal, we numerically simulated NMR relaxometry data and two-phase ﬂow
in rock models with complex pore systems derived from micro-CT images. Speciﬁcally, Navier-Stokes ﬂow in
the partially water-saturated pore space and the evolution of magnetization of the NMR-labeled water mol-
ecules governed by diffusion processes were jointly simulated using an adapted Lattice Boltzmann (LB)
algorithm. The LB method is a well-established class of tools for computational ﬂuid dynamics (CFD) which
can numerically simulate ﬂow in complex geometries. Various model extensions make it possible to also
simulate multiphase ﬂow and advection/diffusion processes in porous media [e.g., Rothmann and Keller ,
1988; Vogel et al ., 2005; T€olke et al ., 2006; Ahrenholz et al ., 2008]. As discussed in subsequent sections, we
employed an extended Rothmann-Keller model coupled with an advection/diffusion model to jointly simu-
late drainage and imbibition processes along with transient NMR T
1 relaxometry data.

3. Lattice Boltzmann Method for Modeling Joint Multiphase Flow and NMR
Relaxation
Computational ﬂuid dynamics (CFD) have proven to be a viable complementary tool for analyzing and pre-
dicting complex transport systems. The LB method is a well-established class of CFD tools that can be used
to numerically simulate ﬂow in complex geometries like porous media. Various model extensions allow the
additional simulation of multiphase ﬂow and advection/diffusion processes. For this work, an extended
Rothmann-Keller model [Rothmann and Keller , 1988] was coupled with an advection/diffusion model using
an LB method [ Stiebler et al ., 2008] to jointly simulate drainage and imbibition processes along with NMR T
1
relaxation. Hereby, we (a) simulated the multiphase ﬂow during drainage or imbibition and (b) solved the
NMR diffusion equation at each saturation level of the system.

3.2. LB Model for NMR Diffusion
By choosing a different collision operator, the LB formalism can also be applied for solving other types of
conservation-based physical models, such as diffusion equations. Like the original LB method for ﬂuid ﬂow, these
methods are well suited for parallelization and for simulating processes in complex geometries. Moreover, they
are advantageous for coupled ﬂow problems where an LB solver has already been used for the ﬂow; this is
because the numerical grid is usually the same as the one used for the ﬂow problem. Thus, coupling tends to be
straightforward.
The LB model we chose for solving the NMR diffusion equation (equation (2)) is a member of a family of
advection/diffusion LB algorithms described in Ginzburg [2005a]. The particular member we chose is an
equilibrium type model, i.e., the diffusivity of the ﬂuid is set via a parameter in the quasi-Maxwellian equilib-
rium distribution rather than via the relaxation rate of the distributions, which is ﬁxed at a value that opti-
mizes the numerical properties of the model. As diffusion is a structurally simpler problem than the Navier-
Stokes equation, a smaller set of macroscopic velocities sufﬁces for its solution. In order to save memory
and ﬂoating point operations, we chose a set of only seven microscopic velocities (D3Q7, Figure 3b),
although the isotropy properties of such a method are compromised. Accordingly, we introduce a set of

tions—valid for fast and slow diffusion regimes—in terms of their respective eigenvalues for cylindrical,

6. Conclusions
A numerical framework was developed that allows for the joint simulation of both the NMR relaxation and
transport properties of partially saturated porous systems. Here, Lattice Boltzmann methods were used for
jointly simulating two-phase ﬂow and NMR T1 relaxometry signatures in a steady state model environment.
The models were validated by analytical solutions for simple spherical pore geometries. Moreover, numerical
experiments were compiled using the complex 3-D pore space derived from micro-CT images. The presented
numerical simulations agree well with laboratory observations regarding porosity, inner surface area, as well
as NMR amplitudes, and decay times for varying water saturation degrees of a packed sand sample.
In this context, the numerical LB experiments can be used to investigate the observed complex behavior of
NMR signatures of partially saturated porous media, such as rocks and soils. In that way, the actual spatial
distribution of the pore ﬂuids in a rock can be simulated and imaged jointly with the NMR relaxometry sig-
natures. Consequently, such numerical experiments can be utilized to conduct numerical experiments
