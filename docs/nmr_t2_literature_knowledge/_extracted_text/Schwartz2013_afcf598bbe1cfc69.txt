Modeling two-dimensional magnetic resonance measurements in coupled pore systems

We present numerical simulations of a two-dimensional (2D) nuclear magnetic resonance process,
T2-storage-T2, on a simple mixed porosity system, the micrograin consolidation ( μGC) model. The results
of these calculations are compared with predictions based on the analytic two-site exchange model, for which
we have independently established numerical values for all the input parameters. Although there is qualitative
and semiquantitative agreement between the two models, we identify speciﬁc instances where the two-site model
fails to properly describe the combined effects of relaxation and diffusion. Generally, these instances occur when
a gradient in magnetization within the large pores of the μGC model is established during the initial phase of the
2D process. The two-site model assumes that the magnetization is spatially uniform within each of its subpore
systems and thus cannot describe such effects.

I. INTRODUCTION
Two-dimensional (2D) nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR)
relaxation experiments have been applied to a wide range of
ﬂuid-ﬁlled porous media including petroleum reservoir rocks
[1–7], bead packs [ 8], cements [ 9,10], and gypsum plaster
[11]. In addition, they have been used to study catalysts [ 12],
surfactants [13], liquid crystals [14,15], and other materials of
interest [16,17].
Two-dimensional NMR relaxation measurements provide a
correlation between the initial (encoding) and ﬁnal (detection)
parts of the experiment. The encoding and detection sequences
may involve longitudinal T
1 or transverse T2, decay. An
important class, including T2-T2 [6,10,18] and T1-T1 [19]
correlations, incorporates a user-controlled storage interval ts
between the encoding and detection intervals. Typically, the
experimental data are processed to produce a 2D distribution
of lifetimes. In the resulting cross plot off-diagonal peaks
may be observed; these have been interpreted, heuristically, in
terms of diffusive exchange between distinct spin populations.
Indeed, by varying t
s , exchange rates or coupling parameters
have been estimated by several authors [ 6–8,10,19]. While
the well-deﬁned storage time is appealing for qualitative
interpretation, there is a continuing controversy as to the link
between the off-diagonal peak amplitudes and t
s .
In this paper we focus on the T2-ts -T2 correlation in model
systems with distinct pore subpopulations. Our calculations
proceed in the time domain; we follow the spin decay from
the initial encoding sequence through the storage period and
into the detection sequence. Our results are easily displayed
as 2D distributions of relaxation lifetimes and we can directly
monitor the dependence of the off-diagonal peaks amplitudes
on the storage time t
s . The ﬁrst model we work with is
a two-site exchange model introduced by Zimmerman and
Brittin [ 20] and by McConnell [ 21]. The model is based
on several critical assumptions, most important that each
spin population, in isolation, exhibits just a single relaxation
mode. Physically, this would be consistent with very weak
surface relaxation as discussed by Brownstein and Tarr [ 22].
However, individual pores in real samples may exhibit multiple
relaxation modes [ 22]. Such behavior can arise for a variety
of reasons, for example, from intermediate or large values
of surface relaxation or from magnetic susceptibility contrast
inducing intrapore ﬁeld gradients [ 6,23]. We turn next to the
micrograin consolidation ( μGC) model. This model is based
on an explicit three-dimensional pore geometry that idealizes
the mixture of macropores and micropores found in many
carbonate grainstones [ 24]. Such textures are important in
oil reservoirs and have been studied experimentally using the
T
2-T2 correlation [7].
The purpose of our analysis is to clarify the interpretation of
T2-ts -T2 measurements. Since the value of 2D measurements is
related to the way that they describe coupling between distinct
pore populations, it will be useful to modulate this coupling
directly in our calculations. This is easily done in both the
two-site exchange andμGC models and we make extensive use
of this feature. In the ﬁrst case we can turn off (i.e., set to zero)
the coupling parameters during any of the three stages of the
measurement. Similarly, in the μGC model we can introduce
a barrier between the macropores and micropores. As in the
two-site exchange model, the barrier can be put in place during
the spin encoding, spin storage, or spin detection parts of
the calculation. With the ability to monitor the coupling, we
show that while the amplitude of the off-diagonal peaks in
the T
2-T2 map is initially proportional to ts , it is not true
that these peaks are wholly controlled by diffusive interaction
during the storage period. Diffusion within a given pore may
already impose a magnetization gradient even during the ﬁrst
(encoding) phase of the T
2-ts -T2 protocol.
Section II of this paper has three parts, a brief review
of NMR diffusion and surface relaxation, a description of
the experimental protocol, and a summary of the equations
underlying the two-site exchange and μGC models. In Sec. III
the results of our calculations are presented. In many respects
the two-site exchange and μGC models yield correlations that
are quite similar. However, when we modulate the diffusive
coupling some interesting differences between the two models
are revealed. In Sec. IV we analyze our results based on the
normal modes of the diffusion equation. We show that there

IV . DISCUSSION
Here we examine in some detail the connection between the
two-site exchange model and the results of our calculations
based on the μGC model. Is the former a useful way to
understand the latter? The ﬁrst question to address is whether
the eigenvalues (the decay rates) can be made to match in the
two systems. Second, we investigate the dependence of the
matrix components of the magnetization on the storage time
t
s . Finally, we look at the effects of modulating the interaction
of the macropores and micropores.
A. Eigenvalues
The eigenvalues that deﬁne the behavior of the two-site
exchange model are given by Eq. (17).F o rt h eT2 part of the
decay process in the μGC model we determine the values of
RA and RB by setting them equal to the decay rates when
there is a barrier in place, i.e., when k = 0. For the case where
ρ2 = 2.0 μm/s these decay rates are seen, in Fig. 6(a) or 6(c),
as the reciprocals of the T2 times where the peaks are located.
From these peaks we get R2A = 1/0.4425 = 2.26 s−1 and
R2B = 1/13.4 = 0.0746 s−1. It is straightforward to compute
the volume of each of the pore subsystems in the μGC model:
VA = 1.28 × 106 μm3 and VB = 1.12 × 106 μm3.
There is only one remaining free parameterk in the two-site
exchange model. As far as we know, there is no direct way
of computing this parameter directly so we determine it by
requiring that the decay rates in the coupled system match.
(See the ﬁnal paragraph of Sec. IV B.) In Fig. 10 we plot
Eq. (17) as a function of k. The dot-dashed horizontal lines are
the decay rates determined from the simulations when there is
no barrier in place [Fig. 6(b) or 6(d)]. If the two-site exchange
model is to have any validity for the μGC system the former
curves should intersect the latter rates at the same value of k
in the two cases and we see that they basically do. We have
k = 4.96 × 10
5 μm3/s for the lower curve and k = 5.80 ×
105 μm3/s for the upper one. Considering the approximate
way in which we have determined the decay rates from the

V . CONCLUSION
The T2-ts -T2 measurements are an important class of 2D
NMR experiments and have been used to study a wide range
of complex materials. The general features ofT
2-T2 cross plots
can be modeled either by analytic calculations or by random
walk simulations. In the limit of weak surface relaxation (often
referred to as the fast diffusion limit) off-diagonal peaks in the
cross plot are the consequence of diffusive exchange during
the storage time t
s . This is the case for materials like cements,
where all the pores are in the fast diffusion limit and are
well mixed when exchange occurs [ 9,10]. More generally,
for intermediate values of surface relaxation, the off-diagonal
peaks are related to both intrapore and interpore diffusion and
the interpretation of the measurement is more complicated.
This is most likely the case in carbonate rocks (and some
sandstones), where macropores are 10–100 μm in diameter,
while the micropores are in the 0.1–1.0 μm range.
In order to clarify the physics underlying the T
2-ts -T2
measurement in mixed pore-size systems, we presented here
the results of numerical simulations for the μGC model. This
model consists of a periodic arrangement of macrograins (and
pores) in which each macrograin is itself porous on a length
scale 10–100 times smaller than that of the macropores. It
was because we used a variable step size algorithm that we
were able to simulate accurately processes that occur on vastly
different time scales. In order to simplify our analysis, we
limited our attention to the case where, during the storage
period, the surface relaxivity ρ
1 vanishes. The resulting T2-T2
contour plot, at each value of the storage time ts ,w a sw e l l
described in terms of a simpliﬁed 2 × 2m a t r i xMij (ts ). We
compared the results of Mij (ts ) deduced from our simulations
against those predicted directly from an analytic two-site
exchange model, for which we independently deduced the
values of all the relevant parameters. Although the general
features of M
ij (ts ) are captured by the two-site model it
does not accurately describe the time over which the matrix
elements decay from their initial values to their asymptotic
values. Within the context of the two-site model this decay time
is simply related to the exchange parameter k. Conversely, if
one were to attempt to estimate k from the observed relaxation
time constant, one would deduce quite the wrong answer. The
issue is that the two-site model attributes relaxation solely
to interpore coupling, whereas the relaxation seen during the
storage period in our simulations is due to both interpore and
intrapore diffusion.
