abstract
Nowadays, most of the efforts in NMR applied to porous media are dedicated to studying the molecular
ﬂuid dynamics within and among the pores. These analyses have a higher complexity due to morphology
and chemical composition of rocks, besides dynamic effects as restricted diffusion, diffusional coupling,
and exchange processes. Since the translational nuclear spin diffusion in a conﬁned geometry (e.g. pores
and fractures) requires speciﬁc boundary conditions, the theoretical solutions are restricted to some spe-
cial problems and, in many cases, computational methods are required. The Random Walk Method is a
classic way to simulate self-diffusion along a Digital Porous Medium. Bergman model considers the mag-
netic relaxation process of the ﬂuid molecules by including a probability rate of magnetization survival
under surface interactions. Here we propose a statistical approach to correlate surface magnetic relaxivity
with the computational method applied to the NMR relaxation in order to elucidate the relationship
between simulated relaxation time and pore size of the Digital Porous Medium. The proposed computa-
tional method simulates one- and two-dimensional NMR techniques reproducing, for example, longitu-
dinal and transverse relaxation times (T
1 and T 2, respectively), diffusion coefﬁcients (D), as well as their
correlations. For a good approximation between the numerical and experimental results, it is necessary to
preserve the complexity of translational diffusion through the microstructures in the digital rocks.
Therefore, we use Digital Porous Media obtained by 3D X-ray microtomography. To validate the method,
relaxation times of ideal spherical pores were obtained and compared with the previous determinations
by the Brownstein-Tarr model, as well as the computational approach proposed by Bergman.
Furthermore, simulated and experimental results of synthetic porous media are compared. These results
make evident the potential of computational physics in the analysis of the NMR data for complex porous
materials.

1. Introduction
Nuclear spin relaxation in conﬁned ﬂuids has been extensively
used for decades to obtain information about the structure and
morphology of porous materials, such as cement, bones, and rocks
[1–3], as well as the ﬂuid molecules migration [4–6]. During the
relaxation process, the nuclei are able to permeate the medium
due to translational diffusion before total magnetization returns
to the thermal equilibrium. Therefore, the magnetization decay
due to relaxation contains information about the way the nuclei
diffused through a porous medium and how they interacted with
its surfaces. All this information is detected through the measure-
ment of longitudinal and transverse relaxation times (T 1 and T 2,
respectively), diffusion coefﬁcients (D), as well as their correlations
(T1–T2,T 2–T2, D–T 2) [7–9].
The multi-dimensional NMR experiments allow investigating
the molecular dynamics of the ﬂuid within porous media. How-
ever, most analyses are based on the linear relationship between
relaxation time and pore size, as deﬁned by Brownstein and Tarr
[10] (henceforth referenced as Brownstein-Tarr when mentioning
their work). As an example, one could mention the determination
of the surface magnetic relaxivity, an important parameter in
obtaining pore size distributions, from a D-T 2 experiment [11,12].
To get this information from the NMR data it is necessary to apply
the linear relationship proposed by Brownstein-Tarr to the

The relaxation time distributions bear the effects of microscopic
dynamics. To explore the NMR response in complex materials, dif-
ferent simulations were developed in the past decades. Mendelson
et al. [13] and Bergman et al. [14], using a lattice random walk
method, simulated the spin relaxation in a saturated porous sys-
tem, reproducing the dynamics and relaxation process, and will
be referenced along the text as Bergman. Later, Arns et al. [15,16]
and Shikhov et al. [17] applied this model with the aid of 3D X-
ray microtomography (micro-CT) images to represent the porous
matrix of different samples, exploring the NMR techniques and
permeability.
Solving the problem of diffusion as a random walk problem is a
well-established problem in the literature, where the method is
based in considering that each walker (molecule, nucleus) under-
goes successive statistically independent displacements [18].I n
this work, the simulation of the nuclear spin magnetization
dynamics in a porous medium was modeled based on the spin
packet concept, presented by Bergman, Arns, and co-workers. This
approach implies that each walker represents a volumetric ensem-
ble of excited nuclear spins. The NMR signal contributions are cal-
culated taking into account the interactions of the nuclei with the
pore walls and among themselves.
To preserve the complexity of translational diffusion through
the porous medium microstructures, micro-CT images were
acquired to represent the Digital Porous Media. Several NMR
experiments can be easily reproduced by the developed method,
such as CPMG, Inversion-Recovery, Exchange and Diffusion, as well
as their correlations [5,7,19,20].
The outcomes can be used to improve the interpretation of the
porous media NMR measurements and correlate them with mor-
phological and physicochemical parameters, such as surface mag-
netic susceptibility, pore size distribution, tortuosity, and
permeability.
This work is organized as follows. In the ﬁrst section, we pre-
sent a brief theory of NMR in porous media with some examples
of the mechanisms that produce relaxation. In the second section,
we describe the computational method and how it is used to sim-
ulate diffusion and relaxation inside the porous media. In the last
section, we present the simulated results and compare them to
the experimental data indicating the strengths and shortcomings
of the method.

2.1. Longitudinal and transverse surface relaxivities: q1 and q2
In this section, we will discuss surface and bulk contributions to
relaxation, disregarding effects on transverse relaxation generated
by magnetic susceptibility present in most materials, especially in
the case of rocks.
According to the Bloembergen-Purcell-Pound (BPP) model [22],
a good approximation for magnetic relaxation of a ﬂuid containing
spin 1/2 nuclei associates longitudinal relaxation to the repopula-

T1. An example of this is bulk water. According to this model, mole-
cules close to the surface may experience dynamic restrictions due
to afﬁnity issues, mainly in the translational and rotational move-

3. Methods
Based on the random walk method (RW) to simulate the trans-
lational diffusion of the nuclei in digital rock samples, we propose a
simple formalism to integrate the computational parameters that
involve NMR magnetization decay and the surface relaxivity. The
sample is described by a 3D digital image obtained by micro-CT,
and segmented in pores and solid materials.
In this work, the magnetization evolution will be simulated
inside a Digital Porous Media obtained from synthetic samples:
Ceramic and Glass Bead. The Digital Porous Media was obtained
using a Bruker SkyScan 1272 micro-CT, with ﬁlters of Al (80 kV,
140 lA) and Al-Cu (90 kV, 120 lA), respectively.
These samples have a clear threshold that identiﬁes the solid
matrix and void space (pores), therefore, in this case, simple ﬁlters
and the Interactive Threshold Method were applied to segment the
Digital Porous Media in Solid and void space (porous) [25]. These
methods were applied using the software provided by SkyScan/
Bruker (CTan).
However, in sandstones and, especially in carbonates, this
threshold is not easy to deﬁne, and it is necessary to use more
sophisticated techniques to process the image and identify the
pores correctly. An example of a ﬁlter method is the noise reduc-
tion algorithm Non-Local Means, which does not interfere signiﬁ-
cantly in the surface of the pores when applied [26]. Other
possible solution, is the identiﬁcation of the interface between
the pore and solid matrix, for example, by a gradient of the image,
where the interface can be locked during the segmentation process
and combined with others advanced methods to provide a better
Digital Porous Medium.

The magnetization decay will be characterized by the two relax-
ation process: bulk and surface relaxation. To connect the compu-
tational model with the bulk and surface relaxation times, we will
ﬁrst introduce the bulk component and later, with similar con-
cepts, demonstrate the modeling of the surface.

4. Results and discussions
To compare the results of our proposed method with that
expected by the Bergman model, we present here a comparison
between the analytical results from the Brownstein-Tarr model
and our simulated results.
Furthermore, we constructed two synthetic samples to perform
the NMR experiments and simulations. The ﬁrst one consists of a
ceramic sample of alumina, where pores are produced by saccha-
rose crystals which burn out at high temperature [30]. The grains
of saccharose are selected by different sieves to control the poros-
ity and size distribution. The second sample is built packing glass
beads and sintering at a controlled temperature until necks are
formed. This process provides a rigid glass beads sample that
ensures it will exhibit the same morphology for both the NMR
experiments and NMR simulations. Both cylindrical samples have
10 mm in diameter and 20 mm length.
For the NMR experiments, it was used a Tecmag LapNMR spec-
trometer and a 0.47 T permanent magnet, at 30 /C176C, with a Larmor
frequency of 20 MHz for 1H nuclei. The samples were saturated
with distilled water, using a vacuum pressure of 90 kP, where the
samples remained in the water for 60 min.

5. Conclusions
We developed and validated a Random Walk method to simu-
late the ﬂuid molecules dynamics inside a porous medium, in the
macroscopic scale, and the corresponding effects on the NMR data.
With the aid of micro-CT images and the above-described NMR
data simulation, we presented an alternative method to estimate
the surface relaxivity and, consequently, the pore size distribution.
While the general methods employed to segment the 3D micro-CT
images take into consideration only the geometric porous media
features, our proposed method considers the molecular diffusion
dynamics within digital porous media, which gives appropriate
correlation between NMR and pore size distribution.
These results indicate the potential of the simulations for
exploring and better understanding the correlations between the
NMR observables, molecular dynamics and the microstructure of
the porous medium. As an example, the explicit correlation
between collision number and pore size (hence surface relaxation
times) expressed in Eqs. (21) and (22) , along with the expression
for ~k, allows a connection between the translational molecular
dynamics ( M found in the simulation) and T 2 distributions mea-
sured experimentally. Based on the porous media images one
could, for example, recover geometry information by mapping col-
lision numbers throughout the entire medium, and connecting
them with T 2 distributions [31]. A similar approach that uses this
kind of reasoning was reported in Ref. [32] where, using the Berg-
man model, the authors establish a relationship between the colli-
sion number mapping and variations in surface relaxivity.
The analysis of the pores radii using the general solution for the
diffusion equation highlights the consideration we need to have
when assuming the validity of the fast diffusion equation. The
errors in estimating the radii when the relaxivity is known will also
affect the estimation of the relaxivity, wettability or permeability
from NMR techniques, which assumes the fast diffusion in the
equations.
The synthetic materials are very important to validate the com-
putational physics methods proposed, and we intend to produce
other porous media with a wide range of pores visible by micro-
CT, as well as different connections and surface relaxivities. We
aim in further studies, with the simulations of the Exchange tech-
niques and synthetic porous media, to correlate NMR with other
microstructures parameters and, consequently, the permeability
properties of porous media [5,6].
